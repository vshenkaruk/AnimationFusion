# Niche playbooks

These are adaptable story patterns, not guarantees of popularity. Research the chosen topic and current high-performing examples for every new video. Keep facts sourced and mark forecasts as inference.

## Motivation and self-improvement

- Start with a recognizable choice or obstacle, then show the action immediately.
- Let a visual progress marker, repeated attempt, timer, or physical before/after carry the change.
- Build toward one useful insight viewers can repeat, not a string of generic slogans.
- Use a character when emotion matters, graphics when the idea is a system or measurable progression, and both when the scene needs explanation and emotion.
- Invite a concrete reflection or one small action. Avoid shame, miracle claims, and guarantees.

## Finance and economics

- Show a sourced number, comparison, worked example, or clearly labeled assumption on screen.
- Explain what the number means and where it comes from; state the source and date in research.md.
- Do not impersonate an expert, invent credentials, present guesses as facts, or give personalized financial advice or stock picks.
- Use animation to explain a mechanism or trade-off, not to make an uncertain claim feel proven.
- Ask viewers to compare options or identify an assumption. Do not imply an investment outcome is certain.

## What If and Did You Know

- State the premise and its rules simply; use a named cast, second-person point of view, or graphics-led demonstration based on the treatment score.
- Escalate in visible steps: a small personal effect, a larger practical win, then a cost, reversal, or surprising consequence.
- Put one verified fact or number at the turn when it helps explain a real-world limit. Cite it; do not invent a scientific result.
- Give viewers a clear audience setting: who is affected, where, and under what rule. Avoid vague global claims.
- End on a payoff or open question, then one relevant CTA. Do not promise a later reveal the creator has not decided to make.

## Reuse and originality

Compare the new angle and key images against the last five available videos in the batch/channel history. Note repeated premises, openings, sets, cast moments, and reveals. Keep useful recurring series or character anchors, but change the episode's central question and visual evidence. If history is unavailable, state that instead of pretending a comparison was made.

Do not add unsourced claims about platform ranking rules or future eligibility dates. Public platform explanations are general and may change; they are not a script formula.
